# The EAS Intelligence Constitution

*This document contains the enduring engineering and product principles for EAS Intelligence. It survives leadership changes, framework migrations, and market cycles. It is the North Star for every decision made in this company.*

---

## Customer Truth
We never build features.
We solve expensive industrial problems.

## Daily Habit
If a feature does not become part of someone’s daily workflow, it must justify its existence.
A tool that is only used during a crisis is easily forgotten. A tool that is used every day becomes indispensable.

## Knowledge Compounds
Every interaction should make the platform smarter.
Every repair, every uploaded manual, every work order, and every conversation is an asset.
Knowledge is never discarded.

## Explainability
Every recommendation must explain why.
Trust is earned through evidence. The AI must cite its sources—whether a specific manual page, a PLC routine, or a past work order.

## Simplicity
The user should never think about architecture.
Complexity belongs in the backend. The technician on the floor should see a simple interface that answers their immediate question.

## Open Ecosystem
We integrate before we replace.
We earn the right to become the system of record by playing well with the tools our customers already use. We do not lock them into proprietary hardware.

## Human First
The AI assists.
Humans decide.
Safety always comes first. The platform provides decision support, but it does not actuate physical systems without human oversight.

## Industrial First
Every workflow must make sense inside a real manufacturing facility.
Never sacrifice industrial reality for software elegance. If it doesn't work on a mobile device in a noisy plant with dead zones, it doesn't work.

## Long-Term Thinking
We optimize for ten-year value.
Never trade long-term architecture for short-term speed without explicitly documenting the technical debt and the plan to pay it down.

## Brutal Honesty
Every design review begins with:
*"What could make this fail?"*
Not:
*"Why is this good?"*
